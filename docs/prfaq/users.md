# Jet Skirmish users

Five people who might use Jet Skirmish. They are hypothetical, written from
the audience the [README](../../README.md) names, and their quotes are
illustrative, not playtest results ([PLAYTEST.md](../PLAYTEST.md)).

### 1. Priya, the returning player
A 23-year-old university student who played Mini Militia over school Wi-Fi
every lunchtime. She uses Jet Skirmish to get that feel back with the same
friends, now spread across different cities and phones.
> "The jetpack feels right: I'm watching my fuel again and diving behind cover.
> And I didn't have to convince anyone to install anything."

### 2. Tom, the newcomer
Priya's flatmate, who rarely plays shooters. He uses it because his friends are
playing, and he needs to get from "what do I press?" to finishing a round
without slowing everyone down.
> "I did Training for five minutes with the bots, and then I actually helped
> carry our flag in. Nobody had to explain the controls to me."

### 3. Sam, the game-night host
Runs a Friday group chat of six or seven friends. Sam uses private rooms with a
password, sets the map and rules, and fills empty seats with bots when someone
is late.
> "I send one code to the chat and we're in. When my Wi-Fi died, the host just
> passed to Mia and the match kept going."

### 4. Aiko, the phone player
Plays on her phone on the bus or from the couch while others are on laptops. She
relies on the twin-stick touch controls and moves the buttons where her thumbs
actually land.
> "I dragged the fire button down and made the stick bigger. Now I can fly and
> shoot without my thumbs covering my own pilot."

### 5. Marcus, the keyboard-only player
Has a wrist injury that makes long mouse sessions painful, and is sensitive to
bright flashes. He uses numpad aiming, remapped keys, low-flash mode and reduced
screen shake.
> "I can aim in eight directions on the numpad and fire without touching the
> mouse, and flashbangs go dark instead of white."
